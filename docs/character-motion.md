# Character motion and shared attention

## What the research supports

- **Apple ELEGNT.** The authors compared functional and expressive motion on a lamp-like robot in six scenarios. In their video study of 21 participants, expressive motion improved perceived character and engagement. Some participants also found continuous or exaggerated motion distracting. Here, the robot briefly anticipates a turn and puts more weight into a meaningful arrival. The pose holds during listening and inspection so the action remains legible. [ELEGNT, Sections 3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12493v1)
- **Disney's interactive gaze.** The animatronic system layers movements with different response speeds and uses salience, habituation and brief glances to decide where to look. This robot applies that separation to its four joints. A directed look temporarily takes priority over idle curiosity, the head leads the turn, and ordinary attention resumes after the hold. [Realistic and Interactive Robot Gaze](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/realistic-and-interactive-robot-gaze/)
- **Disney's BDX and Olaf.** The teams used artist-authored animation references and validated control in simulation and on physical hardware. Olaf's controller also accounts for acoustic impact and actuator heat. This project borrows the workflow of designing character poses, replaying identical scenes and keeping hardware limits explicit. Its spring controller ships authored poses, and its servo-command replay does not measure forces, noise or temperature. [BDX](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/design-and-control-of-a-bipedal-robotic-character/) · [Olaf, Sections III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705v1)
- **MIT and Brown on shared attention.** Brooks and Breazeal address the inherent imprecision of human pointing by combining gesture with speech and object labels. Rosen and colleagues use uncertainty to decide when to ask a question. BITCH combines an addressed utterance with a stable, contemporaneous pointing direction. It asks for a clearer reference when the arm is ambiguous or multiple people are visible. [Brooks and Breazeal](https://robots.media.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2015/01/BrooksBreazeal-hri-06.pdf) · [Rosen et al.](https://cs.brown.edu/people/er35/publications/pvd_iros2020.pdf)

## What runs on the robot

The Mac uses [MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker) to estimate the pointing region from shoulder, elbow, wrist and index landmarks. A gesture must be steady and overlap the spoken request. The preview marks the selected region. The same camera-to-body alignment used for face tracking maps that spot to the Pi's base and neck. It works best at the distance used during alignment. A single RGB camera estimates direction in the image and cannot tell exactly how far along the finger someone intended.

Naming an object sends one current frame to the local vision model and asks for a single visible box. Multiple candidates or an unclear result prompt a follow-up. The Pi accepts only bounded gaze commands. Camera frames and pointing landmarks stay in memory and are excluded from chat history.

## How to evaluate the character

Compare the same interaction with both controllers so that perception latency does not masquerade as better animation. Eleven scripted scenes were used for the movement revision, including conversation pauses, a person leaving view, and several speech styles. The chosen choreography was also run for 360,000 simulated steps with randomized state changes. Commanded travel, speed and acceleration away from the hard position limits were checked against the existing four-joint limits.

For physical evaluation, use the calibrated camera and compare these sequences with the same person and objects. Record whether a nearby observer recognizes the intended beat without sound, whether the held gaze is readable, how long the turn takes, and whether identical or closely spaced objects lead to a clarification. The simulation measures commanded positions. Physical tracking accuracy and how the performance reads to an observer require live observation.

## One performance across speech and movement

The reply now carries a performance choice alongside its words. A sad reply settles into a slower lean. Mock annoyance pitches forward. Surprise pulls back before recovering, and amusement begins with an audible chuckle whose pulses drive motion. The model makes the expression choice, the audio supplies the accent timing, and the Pi runs the selected behavior inside its calibrated limits.

The next step is to give each clause an intent and a target of attention, then compile those into one timed score for voice, gaze, head and body. Perception can contribute a separate reaction between replies. A turn change, interruption, or loss of a tracked target cancels the remaining score. To evaluate the result, replay identical utterances and judge whether the intent is clear with the sound off and whether body accents land with the sound on.

For a new skill, the model can propose a small plan using typed operations such as acquire a visual target, follow it, hold a pose, or play a cue. A validator can check available sensors, time bounds, joint limits, ownership of the camera and cancellation behavior before running the plan. A request that needs a new detector or hardware capability becomes a development task with a recorded fixture and a simulation test before deployment. This gives the model room to improvise new combinations of behaviors when paired with the existing hardware controller.
